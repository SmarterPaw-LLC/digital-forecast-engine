Finding 1 — Nothing is genuinely unprofitable

Ten SKUs show negative Non-Ad Profit, which produces a negative break-even ROAS. All ten sold fewer than 25 units in the window and are flagged accordingly.

The cause is cost allocation, not economics. Storage and inbound fees are divided across units sold in the period. One SKU sold a single unit and absorbed a full quarter of storage on it, producing a per-unit cost of $162 on a $23.99 product.

How to read a negative break-even ROAS

It means the SKU does not cover its costs before advertising. It is not a ROAS target that can be achieved. On flagged low-volume rows, treat it as a measurement artifact rather than a finding.

Actual and expected fees are not on the same rate basis

Across the 69 matched SKUs, the fee Amazon charged runs below its stated expected fee, by a median of $0.70 per unit. Only 5 SKUs were charged more than expected, totalling $44.69 across the window.

Before this column becomes a control

A one-directional gap across 57 of 69 SKUs points at a rate-basis difference rather than mis-measurement. Actual charges cover July through September; the Fee Preview reflects the rate card at the time of export, which in late September can already carry Q4 peak fulfillment rates. Same product, two different rate periods.

The practical consequence: as written, this column reads as a forward signal of what fulfillment will cost, not as an overcharge detector. Aligning the periods, or using Amazon's own historical fee basis, is needed before variance here supports a reimbursement claim.

On genuine overcharges, the exposure found is immaterial. The five cases total under $200 annualized, all on low-volume SKUs. That is a finding worth having, since it rules out a size-tier problem in the Meowijuana catalog rather than leaving it an open question.

How to read the file

  • Minimum volume. Below roughly 25 units per quarter, storage and inbound allocation makes per-unit cost unreliable. Those rows carry a Confidence Flag.
  • Stock on hand includes inbound. Available and inbound are also broken out separately if a narrower definition is preferred.
  • Costing is contribution margin. General overhead is not allocated. Flag if fully absorbed costing is required instead.
  • Break-even is independent of advertising. It is calculated before ad spend, so gaps in the ad columns do not affect it.
  • Velocity divisor. The window is 12 weeks but divides by three months, so average monthly units run about 7% low and stock cover about 7% high.
